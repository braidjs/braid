How it works,
one word at a time

Words you'll hear a lot, in plain English.

Some of these sound like a fantasy novel. They're simpler than they sound.

frag·ment noun
One of the apps on the page. It's a normal web app with its own team and its own deploys. Often it has no Braid code in it at all.
host noun
The app that owns the page. It draws the layout and the menu, and it leaves spaces for the other apps.
slot noun
One of those spaces. You write <fragment-slot name="billing"> and the billing app shows up there. That's all the host has to do.
realm noun
The sealed-off space where an app's code runs. It's invisible, and nothing inside it can reach out and change your page.
de·pend·en·cy graph noun
Every library your apps use, and every library those use. When apps share one, they have to agree on versions. With Braid, each app keeps its own.
con·tract skew noun
When two apps were built at different times and now disagree on what their shared data looks like. Braid spots it, upgrades the data when it can, and says so clearly when it can't.
pierc·ing noun
The server adds each app's content to the page before sending it, so people see it right away instead of watching an empty box.
born in·ert adjective
An app's content arrives switched off. Any scripts in it can't run on your page. They only run inside that app's own realm.
ad·apt·er noun
How Braid starts an app. compat works with apps you can't change. contract is for apps written to work with Braid, and needs no server.
